**Role.** Labour MP for Nottingham South since May 2010. Minister for Innovation, Fraud and Data at the Department for Work and Pensions since 22 July 2026 ([official role](https://www.gov.uk/government/ministers/minister-for-innovation-fraud-and-data-parliamentary-under-secretary-of-state)). On 26 October 2026 she was quoted on the department's first Eligibility Verification Notice, under which banks check relevant benefit receiving accounts against certain eligibility rules and flag cases for the department to look into further. The Government expects the eligibility verification measure as a whole to save £1.2bn by 2030/31 ([DWP announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-unleashes-12-billion-crackdown-as-part-of-anti-fraud-drive)).

**Transport minister (2024 to 2026).** She launched the Road Safety Strategy with the Transport Secretary on 7 January 2026 ([strategy launch](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/thousands-of-lives-to-be-saved-under-bold-new-road-safety-strategy)) and marked ministers' approval of the A47/A11 Thickthorn junction scheme near Norwich in February 2025 ([A47 approval](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ministers-approve-long-awaited-a47-road-scheme-to-support-over-40000-homes-and-30000-new-jobs)). She was also a government whip from September 2025 to July 2026 ([official biography](https://www.gov.uk/government/people/lilian-greenwood)).

**Transport Committee chair (2017 to 2020).** She chaired the committee from July 2017 to January 2020 ([official biography](https://www.gov.uk/government/people/lilian-greenwood)). Its report of 9 September 2019 recommended, in the long term, a ban on pavement parking across England outside London ([committee report](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm201719/cmselect/cmtrans/1982/198202.htm)). The Government consulted in 2020 ([2020 consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/managing-pavement-parking)), and in January 2026, as transport minister, she announced local powers instead ([January 2026 announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-local-powers-to-keep-pavements-clear-for-those-who-rely-on-them-most)).

**In opposition (2010 to 2024).** She was shadow rail minister from 2011 to 2015 and Shadow Transport Secretary from 2015 to 2016; chaired the Commons Finance Committee from 2020 to 2021; was Opposition Deputy Chief Whip from 2021 to 2023; and was a shadow culture minister from 2023 to 2024 ([parliamentary career](https://members.parliament.uk/member/4029/career)). Her 2015 bill on access to on demand television for people with sight or hearing loss had its first reading in June 2015 but did not complete second reading; the motion was objected to on 20 November 2015 ([bill record](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/1642), [Hansard, 20 November 2015](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/2015-11-20/debates/ad583853-8ca1-4103-9805-2e0edaa9efb7/CommonsChamber)).

**Commitments and outcomes, as of 8 November 2026.**

**Pavement parking.**

**Obstruction penalties · Legislation planned for January 2027.** The Department for Transport plans to introduce secondary legislation then letting councils with civil parking enforcement powers issue penalties where a vehicle unnecessarily obstructs the pavement ([27 October written answer](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-10-19/36166)).

**Area wide prohibition · Legislation pending, no date.** The department plans secondary legislation "as soon as possible" to let local transport authorities ban pavement parking across their areas ([26 October written answer](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-10-19/36010)).

**Driving test waits.**

**Deadline missed.** The December 2024 plan she announced set seven weeks by December 2025 ([DVSA plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/dvsa-sets-out-plan-to-reduce-driving-test-waiting-times)); the national average that month was 21.9 weeks ([January written answer](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-01-12/104861)), and the 2025 to 2026 business plan keeps the goal with no date ([DVSA business plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dvsa-business-plan-2025-to-2026/driver-and-vehicle-standards-agency-business-plan-2025-to-2026)).

**Road safety.**

**Target due in 2035.** A 65 per cent cut in people killed or seriously injured on roads in Great Britain, against a 2022 to 2024 baseline ([Road Safety Strategy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/road-safety-strategy)).
