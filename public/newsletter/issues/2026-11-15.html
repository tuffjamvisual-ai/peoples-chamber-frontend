STILL WAITING

25 DAYS AND COUNTING: On 19 October Danny Kruger MP asked the Ministry of Defence a simple question: has it considered writing off the money it is recovering from forces pensioners it overpaid by mistake? Parliament gave the MOD until 21 October to answer. When I checked on 14 November, there was still no answer.

The money: 335 forces pensioners were overpaid £5.1 million because National Insurance adjustments were calculated wrongly. The MOD spotted a possible problem in November 2024 but didn't start telling people until January 2026, and it now wants the money back.

Who it says is responsible: it puts the errors down to Equiniti, which pays the pensions for the scheme's administrator, Sopra Steria. Ministers said on 5 November that investigations into what went wrong, and whether any contract was breached, are still going on. Our full report

Their explanation: why did it take 14 months to tell anyone? This is the government's own answer, from a House of Lords written answer on 22 September (the typo is theirs):

"The delay in notifying affected pension members after initial identification of the issue was cause [sic] by the need to assess the full extent of the error, accurately identify all the affected individuals, and validate cases before corrective action could commence."

That explains the work involved. It doesn't give a timetable, or say why people whose cases were already confirmed couldn't be told sooner. Read the full answer

What we're asking (our view): pensioners can ask for a write off where it applies. The MOD should publish how it decides those requests, make sure everyone affected knows they can ask, and say how many it has granted. I'll check Mr Kruger's question again on 18 November. Track it yourself

Also waiting on: the Ministry of Justice is due to send us the record of the 2008 inspection of the first electronic tagging contracts by 2 December. DWP should answer our narrowed request for Motability figures by council and constituency around 9 December.

THE WIND AUCTION: Ministers nearly doubled the offshore wind budget to £1.79bn a year after seeing anonymised details of the bids that had missed out. The formula they used to cost it doesn't allow for a congested grid. The decision and the warnings
